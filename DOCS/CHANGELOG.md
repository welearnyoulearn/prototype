### Fixed
- **Plan changes and staff seats** (#235): downgrading a school (for example Standard with 5 active staff → Basic, limit 2), or lowering a plan's staff limit, used to be silent — nothing warned the platform admin, nothing told the school, and the school just found out when an Add was refused. Now a change that would leave a school over its limit is refused until the platform admin confirms it, with the exact numbers (active accounts vs the new limit); the same confirmation lists the affected schools when a plan's limit is lowered for everyone. Nobody is ever switched off: the school keeps every account working but can't add or reactivate until it deactivates enough accounts or upgrades. Its administrators get an email and a banner in Settings → Staff Accounts. Deactivating first and then downgrading raises no warning; upgrading frees seats but never reactivates anyone by itself. Every plan change and limit change is now written to the platform audit log with the admin's email, the from/to plan and the seat numbers (the audit page listed "Changed Plan" but nothing was writing it). The plan endpoint also reports `active_staff` and `seats_over`, and the platform Feature Plans page now shows the server's reason when a limit is rejected instead of a bare "Failed to save".
- **Staff accounts hardening** (#234): `PUT/GET /api/schools/{id}/subscription` had no login check and `/api/` is public in the proxy, so anyone could read or change any school's plan (and with it the feature set and staff limit) by id — reading now needs the platform admin or that school's staff, changing needs the platform admin. Creating a staff account trusted the `school_id` in the request body, so any staff login could create a School Administrator inside another school; the school now always comes from the login, and only a School Administrator can add, deactivate or reactivate (a Vice Principal could previously invite a second administrator). Reactivating an account now needs a free seat (deactivate → add someone → reactivate used to go over the plan limit), seats are counted under a per-school lock so two simultaneous adds can't both take the last one, and the check no longer fails open on a database error. The last active School Administrator can't be deactivated, and a platform "reset password" now also reactivates the owner, so a locked-out school can be recovered. A blank ("unlimited") plan limit was reset to the default the next time the schema version changed, and unreadable values typed into the limit box were silently saved as unlimited; limits are now seeded only once, validated (whole number 1–1000 or blank), and saved together with the feature matrix so a failure is reported instead of swallowed. Deactivating an account now voids its unused invite/reset links, deactivated accounts no longer receive password-reset emails or accept reset links, and each create/deactivate/reactivate is recorded with who and when. Settings → Staff Accounts now loads the plan itself (the limit was only known after opening the Plan tab), shows the server's refusal instead of flipping the row anyway, hides actions from roles that can't use them, disables Reactivate when full, and a newly added account no longer shows a Reactivate button until reload. A deactivated email now gets a message pointing to Reactivate. Account-closure/export request emails escape the reason text.
- **Fee module financial-integrity audit fixes**: (1) approving an online payment no longer silently caps the ledger credit while marking the full receipt "completed" — if an offline collection already used up the bill's remaining room, approval is now rejected until the overlap is reconciled, so receipts and the ledger can't drift apart and a later cancellation can't wipe out an unrelated payment; (2) admin payment/waiver creation now verifies the ledger actually belongs to the submitted student, not just the school; (3) correcting a waiver now checks it against *all* other active waivers on the same bill, not just payments, so a correction can no longer push the combined waived total past the bill amount; (4) that same correction now locks the waiver row, closing a race where two concurrent corrections could both apply and corrupt the ledger's waiver total; (5) editing a bill's amount now checks it against payments **and** waivers combined, and locks the row, so a bill can no longer be cut below what's already covered; (6) deleting a ledger entry is now blocked whenever *any* payment or waiver history exists for it (including cancelled/revoked/pending rows), not just the current running totals — those rows were being cascade-deleted along with the bill; (7) carrying a balance forward a second time (e.g. after reopening a closed year and cancelling a payment) now adds to the existing carried-forward bill instead of overwriting it; (8) year-end apply now locks the ledger rows it's closing out, so a payment collected mid-run can no longer leave year-end carrying forward or writing off a stale balance; (9) a parent paying several bills at once no longer double-allocates to a bill that already has a payment awaiting verification; (10) resubmitting a day's cash reconciliation now recalculates every total (not just the actual-cash difference), so the saved record can't go stale after more collections come in; (11) online payment submission and approval no longer risk timing out on Vercel's single-connection pool by requesting a second connection while already holding the only one. **Follow-up review found the first pass incomplete in four places, now also fixed:** the pool-deadlock fix (11) missed `requireFeeAccess`'s own session-validation query — every fee route that calls it after already holding a connection (payment verify/cancel, waiver correct/revoke, ledger edit) could still self-deadlock on Vercel; a payment cancellation could still race a concurrent year-end close and resurrect debt in an already-closed year (the closed-year check ran before the transaction's row lock, so it could act on stale state); carrying a balance forward only checked that the destination year *exists*, not that it's still open, so a reopened source year's dues could be carried into an already-closed destination year; and two of the five new regression tests (`WVCOR-101`, `DAYCLOSE-101`) built their test bill for the wrong grade (9 instead of studentC's actual grade 8) and used `if (!entry) return` with no assertion on the setup calls, so they could pass without exercising anything — all five now assert every setup response and fail loudly if the target bill doesn't exist. **A third review found four more gaps, now also fixed:** the same missing-`client` pool-deadlock pattern was still present in category update/delete, category-assignments and structure amendments (fixed the same way, `requireFeeAccess(id, client)`); an explicit year-end **close** doesn't lock any ledger row (it only writes an aggregate), so the earlier row-lock fix only serialized cancellation against year-end *apply*, not against a plain *close* — payment cancellation, payment/waiver creation, waiver correction/revocation and ledger edit/delete now all take the same `pg_advisory_xact_lock` (`lockYearClose`) year-end itself uses, before re-checking closed-year state; carrying a balance forward locked only the *source* year, leaving a gap where the *destination* year could be closed between the check and the insert — now locks both; and the five new regression tests asserted `structRes.status` as 200 (the endpoint actually returns 201) and built their bills in `AY`, which is closed and structure-locked by the point in the suite where these tests run (an earlier `/api/fees/generate` call auto-locks the plan, and `YE-010` closes the year with no later reopen) — all five now use `AY_NEXT` (created for the carry-forward tests, never locked or closed) and assert 201. **A fourth review found three more gaps:** structure amendments, variable-fee assignments and bill generation still checked closure without holding `lockYearClose` (only the row-locking routes had been converted) — all three now take it, in the same "year lock first" order as everything else; the newly-added locks introduced an ordering inconsistency of their own — payment verification and waiver correction locked their own row *before* the year, while cancellation and waiver revocation locked the year *before* their row, so a concurrent approve-vs-cancel or correct-vs-revoke on the same payment/waiver could deadlock (Postgres safely aborts one side, but it's a needless retry) — every route now takes `lockYearClose` first, consistently; and switching the five regression tests to the shared `AY_NEXT` only moved the shared-state problem onto itself (the first test's own `/api/fees/generate` call auto-locks `AY_NEXT` for every test after it, and filtering to just these five tests via `--grep` skips `YE-004`, which is what creates `AY_NEXT` at all) — each of the five now creates and uses its own dedicated academic year (`testYear(1)`–`testYear(5)`, year labels far in the future), making them fully independent of `AY`, `AY_NEXT`, `YE-004`, and each other. **A fifth review found one more gap:** bill generation read the year's fee structures and variable assignments (the actual amounts it bills) via the autocommit pool *before* acquiring `lockYearClose`, not after — so a fee-structure amendment that committed in that window was silently ignored, generating bills at the pre-amendment amount while reporting success. All three reads (structures, variable-category assignments, the student list) now happen inside the transaction, through the locked connection, after `lockYearClose` — the same fix already applied to every other mutating fee route in the earlier rounds, just missed here because this route reads billing data before it writes it, which the earlier passes didn't specifically check for.
- **Fee module audit, wider scope (broader source review beyond the original 11 findings)**: a student or teacher session could read or submit a payment against ANY same-school student's fees via `/api/parent/fees` — the route only checked the parent-link relationship when the role was literally `parent`, letting the other two roles (neither of which has any such link to check) straight through; it's now parent-only, matching its only real caller. Bill generation's grade-resync path floored a resynced amount against `amount_paid` alone, the same class of bug already fixed elsewhere for payments/waivers, letting a heavily-waived bill's amount_due drop below what's already covered. The ledger-amount PATCH accepted the literal string `"NaN"` — every `<=`/`<` comparison against it evaluates to false, so it sailed past every guard into the database, and Postgres's own `>= 0` CHECK constraint doesn't exclude the special numeric value NaN either (it sorts above every finite number); both now explicitly reject it, and require two-decimal precision. A variable-fee reduction's overpay check kept only the LAST of several monthly ledger rows sharing a `student:category` key (a stale `Map` collision), so a category billed across multiple periods could be reduced below what one of those periods had already collected. Removing a variable-fee assignment could delete a ledger row — and, through it, cascade-delete linked payment/waiver history — without checking for that history first, the same gap already closed on the direct ledger-delete route. Duplicate-student cleanup's "already has this bill" check compared a column to itself (an unaliased self-join subquery resolved to its own inner scope, not the outer row being updated), making it a tautology that blocked the ENTIRE remap once the kept record had any ledger row at all — payments/waivers got their `student_id` reassigned regardless, but the ledger rows those very payments depended on (via `ledger_id`, `ON DELETE CASCADE`) were left behind and destroyed when the duplicate was deleted; cleanup now correlates the check correctly and refuses to delete a duplicate that still has any real financial history attached. A passout bill's status wasn't recalculated when more debt was added to an already-settled one, hiding a real balance from the passout list's outstanding-only filter. Year-end's carried/write-off counters were replaced by each apply batch instead of accumulating, so a second partial batch could zero out an earlier one's totals. Two `document.write`-based print paths (the admin audit/student report, and the parent receipt) interpolated stored names/reasons/references into raw HTML unescaped; both now escape every text field. Day-close's saved totals and receipt count/range came from two separate autocommit reads that could straddle an intervening payment; now one query, one snapshot. The passbook timeline double-counted every amendment (using the bill's current, already-amended amount as the base, then separately replaying each historical delta on top); it now reconstructs the original amount first. A category report's discretionary-waiver fallback couldn't distinguish "the query failed" from "this category legitimately has none," so a carry-only category reported its whole carried-forward total as a discretionary waiver that was never granted. The parent portal's fee summary counted waivers from every year while every other figure on the same screen was scoped to the one selected year. The parent-side single-bill payment form never sent the structured transaction reference the multi-bill form did. And the parent receipt printout showed only the ONE allocation row that was clicked as if it were the whole receipt, even though several allocations can share one receipt number — it now groups and totals every completed allocation under that receipt.
- **UI gaps from the fixes above**: two of them added new information to an API response that the screen calling it was silently dropping. Duplicate-student cleanup (F09) can now finish "successfully" while still skipping individual duplicates that have real financial history — the Students → Duplicates panel only ever showed a full-request error banner, with no way to see a partial skip; it now shows a result banner naming each skipped student and why. - Admin, teacher, parent and student screens each computed attendance % differently (morning only, late counted as absent, late as half…). They now share one rule, so the same child shows the same numbers everywhere. (#153)

### Changed
- **Year Rollover is now the one central place** to move the whole school to the next academic year (#199). It is blocked until Fee Management → Year-End is closed (popup on screen, `409 FEES_NOT_CLOSED` on the server; schools without Fee Management are not gated). Fee Year-End no longer creates academic years or has its own "Start Year Rollover" action (`POST /api/fees/year-rollover` removed), and a fee year cannot be reopened once rolled over. After a school's first year, the active year can only change through Year Rollover; creating or switching years now needs a school-admin login.
- Year Rollover keeps class roll numbers (it used to fail on the unique roll-number index when, for example, grade 9 roll 1 moved into grade 10 roll 1). The old roll number is stored in the class history; graduated students' roll numbers are cleared; students whose number is already taken in the new class are reported.
- Attendance percentage everywhere = (present + late) ÷ marked sessions; holidays and weekly-off days are excluded; unmarked days are gaps, not absences. (#153)
- Schools now have a weekly-off setting (default Sunday). (#153)

### Added
- Daily Supabase → Cloudflare R2 backup: Vercel Cron (`02:30 UTC`) → `POST /api/cron/backup` streams every public table row-by-row through gzip into an R2 multipart upload (`db/supabase-<ts>.jsonl.gz` + `db/latest.json`) so memory stays flat regardless of DB size; retains the newest 14 backups (paginated listing + batched deletes). - Non-destructive restore: `POST /api/restore` re-inserts only rows missing from the live DB (matched by primary key) via `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`; supports `dryRun`; never updates/deletes/truncates. Tables without a primary key are skipped and reported. (#NN)
